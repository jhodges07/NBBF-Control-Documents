==================================================
1. PURPOSE
==================================================

This document defines the formal ontology of the Node-Based Budget Framework.

It establishes:
• What constitutes a budget Node
• Classification of Nodes by decision authority
• Mandatory structural ownership, authority, cost, signaling, and exit fields
• Structural constraints that prevent pooling, drift, concealment, and
  hostage budgeting

No budget authority, execution authority, or funded obligation may exist
as an NBBF Node outside this ontology.

This ontology operates subject to CONTROL 00. It is not a second Root.

==================================================
2. HUMAN OWNERSHIP
==================================================

Root CONTROL 00 establishes Human Accountability as a foundational principle.

This CONTROL owns Node ontology relating to human ownership.

2.1 Human Node

All Nodes ultimately descend from a Human Node.

A Human Node is a real, named individual who:
• Bears legal responsibility
• Bears budget responsibility
• Publicly defends tradeoffs
• Exercises judgment under uncertainty
• Can authorize continuation, modification, or termination
• Can say “stop”

A Human Node is the ontological origin of Node descent. It is not
CONTROL 00 / NBBF Root Authority.

2.2 Human Owner, Executor, and Guardian

A Human Owner is the single accountable named individual for a Node.

Executor and guardian principles are established by CONTROL 00.
This CONTROL uses those principles as ownership ontology: every Human Owner
serves as both executor and guardian of the public trust attached to that Node.

Automation, committees, algorithms, agencies, CDT, UNBKE, APIs, ledgers,
artificial intelligence, and other digital systems MAY assist implementation.
They SHALL NOT replace human ownership, lawful human judgment, or
constitutional authority.

Concise vocabulary for Human Owner: CONTROL 02.

==================================================
3. DEFINITION OF A NODE
==================================================

A Node is the smallest indivisible unit of budget authority or obligation.

A Node is:
• A bounded decision or obligation
• With a single accountable Human Owner
• A defined purpose
• Explicit inputs and outputs
• Measurable cost or resource claim
• Explicit exit conditions
• An associated signaling surface

A Node is not:
• A department
• A political category
• A pooled fund
• A placeholder for future discretion

Concise vocabulary: CONTROL 02.
Pooling operating doctrine: CONTROL 00C.

==================================================
4. NODE CLASSES BY DECISION AUTHORITY
==================================================

Not all Nodes are subject to Decision-Node authorization.

Nodes are classified by decision authority, not merely by depth.

Jurisdiction-specific application of these classes: CONTROL 03.

--------------------------------------------------

4.1 Decision Nodes (Voting Nodes)

Decision Nodes are Nodes that require affirmative authorization by the
applicable legislative or other constitutionally designated Decision-Node
authority of that jurisdiction.

Characteristics:
• Subject to Decision-Node authorization
• Establish scope, ceilings, and constraints
• Allocate authority downward to child Nodes
• Fewer in number and higher in the tree
• Collapse all dependent child Nodes upon failure

Typical Decision Nodes include:
• Root Nodes (Node-hierarchy type; not CONTROL 00)
• Major Branch Nodes
• Program Nodes designated by statute or rule

The Decision-Node authority authorizes decisions, not execution details.

Congress is an example of a Decision-Node authority in the United States
federal legislative jurisdiction. Congress is not the universal or exclusive
NBBF Decision Node.

Concise vocabulary: CONTROL 02.

--------------------------------------------------

4.2 Assembly and Detail Nodes (Non-Voting Nodes)

Assembly and Detail Nodes decompose approved decisions into executable
structure.

Characteristics:
• Are not themselves the Decision-Node authorization
• Exist to expose cost structure, obligations, and execution detail
• Owned and defended by executive or operational owners
• Automatically collapse if the parent Decision Node loses authority
• Cannot expand scope beyond parent constraints

Typical Assembly and Detail Nodes include:
• Operational Nodes
• Support Nodes
• Cost decomposition Nodes
• Execution and logistics Nodes

Visibility does not imply Decision-Node authorization.

==================================================
5. REQUIRED PROPERTIES OF EVERY NODE
==================================================

Every Node SHALL explicitly declare the following properties.

--------------------------------------------------

5.1 Identity
• Unique Node ID
• Plain-language Node Name
• Node Type
• Node Class (Decision or Assembly/Detail)

--------------------------------------------------

5.2 Ownership
• Single Human Owner (name and role)
• Parent Node in the authority chain

--------------------------------------------------

5.3 Constitutional Authority
• Enumerated constitutional authority citation, or
• Explicit designation as non-enumerated authority
• Scope limitation statement

This field identifies lawful authority for the Node. It does not replace
constitutional government.

--------------------------------------------------

5.4 Cost and Resources
• Authorized-cycle funding request or allocation
• Unit cost assumptions
• Acceptable variance thresholds

Cost and value mechanics: CONTROL 06.
The four-month cycle remains existing NBBF vocabulary. Review-calendar
mechanics: CONTROL 05.

--------------------------------------------------

5.5 Inputs
• Resources consumed
• Preconditions required

--------------------------------------------------

5.6 Outputs and Obligations
• Deliverables, capabilities, or services promised
• Observable expectations downstream

--------------------------------------------------

5.7 Performance Metrics
• Objective performance indicators
• Reporting cadence aligned to the authorized cycle
• Pass/fail criteria

Audit and performance operating standards: CONTROL 07.

--------------------------------------------------

5.8 Signaling Surface (Structural)

Every Node SHALL declare the following structural Signal associations:
• Who is downstream of the Node
• What authorized outcomes are visible to them
• The reporting channel for variance
• The escalation path for unresolved variance

Nodes MAY produce Signals, receive Signals, be associated with Signals,
and participate structurally in the Signal system.

Reporting rights, reporting duties, suppression rules, escalation procedure,
evidence rules, close-out, interference consequences, Signal lifecycle, and
Signal review: CONTROL 00B.

This CONTROL SHALL NOT independently establish those operating rules.

--------------------------------------------------

5.9 Exit Conditions (Structural)

Every Node SHALL declare:
• Automatic termination triggers
• Review or suspension triggers
• Clawback-condition fields where applicable

Operating Exit and Disengagement: CONTROL 00D.
Recovery and clawback mechanics: CONTROL 08.

A Node missing any required property is invalid and ineligible for funding.

==================================================
6. ALLOWED NODE TYPES (TAXONOMY)
==================================================

Only the following Node types are permitted.

--------------------------------------------------

6.1 Root Node (Node-hierarchy type)

A Root Node is an ontology type. It is not CONTROL 00 / NBBF Root Authority.

A Root Node:
• Represents a constitutional authority domain in the Node tree
• Provides scope boundaries
• Holds no funding authority

--------------------------------------------------

6.2 Branch Node
• Organizes related Nodes
• Holds no pooled funding
• Exists for structure and navigation

Pooling operating doctrine: CONTROL 00C.

--------------------------------------------------

6.3 Program Node
• Represents a discrete government function
• SHALL decompose into Assembly/Detail Nodes
• SHALL NOT retain unallocated funds

--------------------------------------------------

6.4 Operational Node
• Executes a specific activity
• Holds direct cost and obligations
• Primary unit of execution accountability

--------------------------------------------------

6.5 Support Node
• Enables Operational Nodes
• Justified only by dependent Nodes
• Automatically collapses if dependencies fail

Exit of consuming Nodes: CONTROL 00D.

--------------------------------------------------

6.6 Legacy Node
• Represents pre-existing obligations
• Scope is sealed and non-expansive
• Subject to sunset and termination rules

Operating exit, sunset, and disengagement: CONTROL 00D.

No other Node types are allowed.

==================================================
7. STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINTS
==================================================

These are ontology constraints. They do not replace specialized operating
CONTROLs.

--------------------------------------------------

7.1 No Pooling (Structural)

• No Node may fund multiple unrelated purposes
• No aggregation of discretionary authority inside one Node
• No obscuring of cost attribution

Pooling operating doctrine, consent, and property-right mechanics:
CONTROL 00C.

This CONTROL SHALL NOT independently create pooling authority, compulsory
pooling doctrine, or consent rules for pooling.

--------------------------------------------------

7.2 Single Ownership
• One Node, one Human Owner
• Committees review; they do not own

--------------------------------------------------

7.3 Default Zero
• All Nodes default to zero funding
• Funding exists only by affirmative approval
• No baselines, auto-renewals, or silent extensions

--------------------------------------------------

7.4 Independence
• Failure of one Decision Node does not affect others
• No cascading failures
• No hostage leverage across Nodes

==================================================
8. NODE LIFECYCLE (STRUCTURAL)
==================================================

This section is high-level ontology. Operating exit: CONTROL 00D.
Legislative Decision-Node interface mechanics: CONTROL 10.

--------------------------------------------------

8.1 Creation
• Fully specified before consideration
• Publicly visible at the appropriate clearance level

Publication, API, and ledger mechanics: CONTROL 11.

--------------------------------------------------

8.2 Defense
• Decision Nodes defended before the applicable Decision-Node authority
• Assembly/Detail Nodes defended by owners and auditors
• Defense is recorded and archived

Federal legislative example: defense before Congress. That example does not
define Decision-Node defense for every jurisdiction.

--------------------------------------------------

8.3 Funding
• Approved on the authorized cycle
• Line-item approval at Decision Node level only

--------------------------------------------------

8.4 Monitoring
• Performance tracked against declared metrics
• Variance evaluated each cycle
• Nodes participate structurally in the Signal system

Signal operating rules: CONTROL 00B.

--------------------------------------------------

8.5 Termination
• Triggered by declared exit conditions
• Clean and isolated collapse of dependent Nodes

Operating termination, disengagement, and closeout: CONTROL 00D.

==================================================
9. ONTOLOGY INVARIANTS
==================================================

The following conditions are always true of NBBF Nodes:

• No spending without defense
• No authority without ownership
• No ownership without exits
• No exits without enforcement
• No pooled power
• No hostage budgeting
• Nodes participate in the Signal system; suppression rules: CONTROL 00B

10. ENFORCEMENT
==================================================

Ontology noncompliance includes:

• Creating or funding a Node that lacks required properties
• Treating a committee, office, process, or machine as the Human Owner
• Expanding Node scope beyond parent constraints
• Using this CONTROL to create pooling, Signal-operation, or exit-procedure
  authority owned elsewhere
• Treating this CONTROL as NBBF Root Authority

Ontology defects MAY be identified by:

• Authorized NBBF CONTROL stewardship under CONTROL 00
• Signals under CONTROL 00B
• Audit under CONTROL 07

Detailed audit procedure: CONTROL 07.
Operating exit consequences: CONTROL 00D.
This CONTROL does not create an enforcement office, tribunal, board,
penalty schedule, policing authority, or new governmental power.
